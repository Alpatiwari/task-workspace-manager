# Part 2 — Exploring AbleSpace's Take Data Workflow

I logged into AbleSpace, went to the Caseload tab, and clicked "Take Data" on
one of the demo students to see how the whole flow works. Here's what I found,
in my own words.

## Getting into a session

On the Caseload tab, every student has a "Take Data" button next to them. I
clicked it for Demo Student1. That opened up a session screen, and I noticed
it automatically started a timer at the top (something like "08/20/2026,
01:43 PM - 02:13 PM"). I'm guessing that's tracking how long the session runs,
maybe for billing or service-time logging later.

## The Goals list

On the left side, there's a list of all the student's IEP goals — it showed
"Goals (7/7)", so this student had 7 goals total, things like Social Studies,
Writing, Math, Reading, Toileting, and Behavior. Each one is a card with a
short title and part of the goal description (the full text gets cut off,
which I'll mention below). Clicking a goal loads its details on the right
side of the screen.

## Actually taking data

I clicked into the first goal — "Social Studies," where the target was
"Demo Student1 will take 5 bites, from a handheld snack, such as a cheese
stick, in 4 out of 5 opportunities." On the right there's a Capture tab with
a big counter box, and below it a big "+" button. Every time I tapped the +,
the number went up by one — I tapped it twice and it showed "2." There's also
an "Undo" link above the counter if you tap by mistake.

What I liked: as soon as I logged some data, a green checkmark appeared on
that goal in the sidebar list, and also on the student's name tab at the top.
So at a glance you can tell which goals still need data taken in this session
and which ones are done.

There's also a Notes section right below the counter, with an "Add Note"
button and a "Load Last Note" shortcut, plus a separate "Lesson Plan" tab —
so you can jot down context without leaving the data screen.

## Seeing progress over time

Next to Capture, there are three more tabs: Graph, Stats, and Info. I clicked
Graph, and it plotted every data point that's been logged for that goal over
time — dates along the bottom, and the count per session on the vertical
axis. The line went up from 1 all the way to 5 over a few months, then
dropped down to 2 in the latest session. It's a really quick way to see if a
student is trending toward their goal or slipping, without having to build a
chart yourself.

I didn't dig deep into Stats or Info, but based on the tab names I'd guess
Stats shows numeric summaries (like mastery percentage) and Info shows the
goal's setup details (baseline, target, dates).

## Things I'd improve

A few things stood out to me while clicking around:

1. **Goal titles get cut off.** In the sidebar, most goal names show as
   something like "1. Social Studi..." or "3. Ma...", so you can't tell what
   the goal actually is without clicking in or hovering. A tooltip on hover,
   or just a wider sidebar, would fix this pretty easily.

2. **Undo only goes one step back.** If you accidentally tap "+" a few times
   in a row, there's no way I could find to undo more than the last tap. A
   small history of recent taps with the ability to remove a specific one
   would help during a fast-paced session.

3. **The green checkmark is a little ambiguous.** It shows up once you've
   logged any data for a goal, but at first glance I actually thought it
   meant the goal was "completed" or "mastered." Maybe a different icon (like
   a filled dot) for "data logged today" vs. an actual checkmark for
   "mastered" would avoid that confusion.

4. **No obvious way to end/edit the session timer.** The session start and
   end time show at the top, but I couldn't find a clear button to manually
   end the session or adjust the time if it ran short or long.

5. **Graph defaults to "All Time."** That's useful for a big-picture view,
   but most of the time I'd probably want to see just the last month or two
   — especially before a quick check-in — so a smarter default range could
   save a click.

## Overall

The core workflow is genuinely fast — open a student, pick a goal, tap a
button per trial, and you're done. That's exactly what a teacher running a
lesson needs, since they don't want to be fumbling with an app while working
with a kid. Most of what I'd change is around small clarity issues (cut-off
titles, checkmark meaning, session controls) rather than the main flow
itself, which already feels solid.
